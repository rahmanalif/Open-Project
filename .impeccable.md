## Design Context

### Users
Open Project is for people looking for collaborators on creative and technical work: developers, designers, product managers, writers, QA, marketers, and adjacent roles. They use the product when they want to find strong-fit collaborators quickly, create project listings, and then manage the collaboration once a project is underway.

The product matters especially for people who are thoughtful, independent, or introverted and may find it difficult to confidently put themselves forward, ask seniors for help, or pitch unfinished ideas. The interface should lower that emotional barrier and make collaboration feel safe, credible, and inviting.

### Brand Personality
Smart, trustworthy, collaborative.

The emotional goal is to make people feel capable and respected. The interface should communicate seriousness and care, with a refined system-like edge in matchmaking, without becoming flashy, noisy, or intimidating.

### Aesthetic Direction
The product should feel like premium collaboration software with a subtle futuristic layer. Dark mode should be the more distinctive mode for matchmaking and discovery, while light mode should feel calm, focused, and productive for everyday workspace usage.

Reference direction: thoughtful productivity software in the spirit of Linear or Notion, blended with a refined signal-processing or systems aesthetic for matching.

Anti-references: it should not resemble a dating app, a generic freelance marketplace, or a neon crypto dashboard. Avoid hype, swiping metaphors, and noisy “AI sci-fi” tropes.

### Design Principles
1. Reduce social friction: every important screen should make collaboration feel approachable, credible, and low-pressure.
2. Signal quality, not hype: use refined contrast, strong typography, and deliberate motion instead of loud gradients or gimmicks.
3. Make matching feel intelligent: matchmaking surfaces can be more atmospheric and future-facing, but still grounded in clarity and trust.
4. Preserve productivity: listings, workspace, and settings should feel organized, useful, and calm in both light and dark themes.
5. Design for confidence: interfaces should help users feel taken seriously even when their ideas are early or imperfect.
